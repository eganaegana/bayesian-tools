But careful: it doesn't mean the neuron "says blue". Its effect on the final prediction also depends on the sign of its outgoing weights: through a negative weight, "blue" activity pushes the prediction toward orange.

How to read the screen
  • Boxes are neurons; their colour is activity: positive, negative, pale near zero. Activity changes with every spot you pick.
  • Lines are connection weights. Thicker means bigger. solid lines are positive (the activity pushes the next neuron the same way); dashed lines are negative (it pushes the opposite way). Weights change only while it trains, or when you set them by hand.
  • Blue and orange mean a colour: the dots' true colours, and the prediction, which is the output neuron's activity read as a chance of blue.
  • Surprise measures how surprised the network is by a dot's true colour. 0 means it was sure and right; 1.0 is a coin flip; big numbers mean confidently wrong. Training nudges every weight to lower the average surprise on the practice dots.
