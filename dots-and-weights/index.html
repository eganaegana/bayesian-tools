Blue/orange view: each neuron's positive activation is shown blue and negative activation orange, as if it were making its own prediction. That can help you ask "what pattern is this neuron picking out?" But careful: it doesn't mean the neuron "says blue". Its effect on the final prediction also depends on the sign of its outgoing weights: through a negative weight, "blue" activation pushes the prediction toward orange.

How to read the screen
  • Boxes are neurons; their colour is activation (a number between −1 and 1): positive, negative, pale near zero. Activation changes with every spot you pick.
  • Lines are connection weights. Thicker means bigger. solid lines are positive (the activation pushes the next neuron the same way); dashed lines are negative (it pushes the opposite way). Weights change only while it trains, or when you set them by hand.
  • Blue and orange mean a colour: the dots' true colours, and the prediction, which is the output neuron's activation interpreted as a chance of blue.
  • Surprise measures how surprised the network is by a dot's true colour. 0 means it was sure and right; 1.0 is a coin flip; big numbers mean confidently wrong. Training nudges every weight to lower the average surprise on the practice dots.
